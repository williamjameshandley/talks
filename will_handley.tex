\begin{frame}
    \frametitle{JAX ports as standard practice}
    \framesubtitle{Reference-guided translation with a mature oracle}
    \student{charlotte_priestley.jpg}{Charlotte Priestley}{PhD}
    \vspace{-0.5em}
    \begin{columns}[T]
        \column{0.55\textwidth}
        \small
        \begin{itemize}
            \item \texttt{emcee} in BlackJAX: months by hand $\to$
                \textbf{15 minutes} supervised. \hfill \github{blackjax-devs/blackjax}
            \item \texttt{jaxwavelets}: PyWavelets reproduced to
                $10^{-14}$ across \textbf{1,177 tests} in 4.7 hours;
                65 external reviews, 60\% rejected before merge.
                \hfill \github{handley-lab/jaxwavelets}
            \item \texttt{jaxsgp4}: satellite orbit propagation,
                \textbf{1500$\times$}. \hfill \github{cmpriestley/jaxsgp4}
            \item \texttt{jax-lens}, \texttt{jaxPOSEIDON},
                \texttt{jim-emulators} (empty repo $\to$ package, 8 hours);
                a K2-18b retrieval built live in a meeting.
        \end{itemize}
        \column{0.43\textwidth}
        \begin{exampleblock}{The pattern}
            The specification already exists: the reference implementation.
            Every numerical decision is checkable to machine precision.
        \end{exampleblock}
        \begin{alertblock}{}
            \textit{``I wrote no code, but made the scientific decisions.''}
        \end{alertblock}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
    \frametitle{Astro Legacy Archive}
    \framesubtitle{The measurements of astronomy are missing from the ML data ecosystem}
    \student{james_alvey.jpg}{James Alvey}{EuCAIF SBI WG}
    \vspace{-0.5em}
    \begin{columns}[T]
        \column{0.55\textwidth}
        \small
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Hugging Face audited: \textbf{2,130} datasets, 449 astronomy.
                Objects and catalogues only. No mission sky map at any
                wavelength, no visibilities, no event lists, no posterior
                chains; one broken strain fragment.
            \item Each dataset a \keyterm{rebuildable projection} of its
                canonical archive: Parquet, HATS conventions, provenance,
                checksums, licence, and the regeneration command in the card.
            \item Access: LAMBDA at 1.1\,MB/s; the HF CDN at 103.6\,MB/s.
                WMAP's 626\,GB: a week, or under two hours.
            \item Pilot: Haslam, WMAP, COBE, IRAS, ROSAT maps; the complete
                EHT visibilities. Pre-registered success criteria.
        \end{itemize}
        \column{0.43\textwidth}
        \begin{block}{Delivered by agents, judged by a scientist}
            130 dataset cards written; $\sim$2.5\,TB uploaded.\\[0.4em]
            The human decisions are exactly the ones that matter:
            \textit{``Do not normalise. You'll only be normalising for this
            set, not for the sets that come after.''}
        \end{block}
        \begin{exampleblock}{}
            No scientist would have spun up a team to do this. That is the
            point.
        \end{exampleblock}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
    \frametitle{Checking the exam}
    \framesubtitle{The case where the frontier route was not just unnecessary but impossible}
    \vspace{-0.5em}
    \begin{columns}[T]
        \column{0.55\textwidth}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Exam papers cannot leave the building.
            \item Two RTX PRO 6000s in the office; Qwen 3.5 and Gemma 4;
                no internet.
            \item The models found errors a human examiner had missed, and
                went toe to toe with the checker on the same paper.
            \item Nothing about this needed a frontier model. It needed a
                harness, tokens, and a room the paper could stay in.
        \end{itemize}
        \column{0.43\textwidth}
        \begin{block}{Reported, not shown}
            The material is confidential. The pipeline is the same one that
            runs the rest of this talk.
        \end{block}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
    \frametitle{LSST transients: the agent explains, the expert decides}
    \framesubtitle{Forward reference: Nikhil Sarin, this session}
    \student{james_alvey.jpg}{James Alvey}{with Nikhil Sarin}
    \vspace{-0.5em}
    \begin{columns}[T]
        \column{0.55\textwidth}
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Redback + BlackJAX: transient inference in \textbf{85
                minutes on one consumer GPU}.
            \item The bottleneck moves from inference to the small class of
                decisions made on its output.
            \item First attempt: ask the agent to classify. Abandoned.
                \textit{``That isn't the question.''}
            \item The question: \keyterm{does this need reanalysis, and why?}
                Answered in the words a student would use, across
                50--70 thousand events.
            \item Runs on a 16\,GB card, with Gemma.
        \end{itemize}
        \column{0.43\textwidth}
        \begin{exampleblock}{Simple judgement at scale}
            Natural language is how you handle a fuzzy decision boundary
            ten thousand times a week. Nobody wants the job of looking at a
            million corner plots.
        \end{exampleblock}
    \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
    \frametitle{The pattern}
    \framesubtitle{Grunt at scale; interpretation human}
    \student{natalie_hogg.jpg}{Natalie Hogg}{PDRA}
    \vspace{-0.5em}
    \begin{columns}[T]
        \column{0.48\textwidth}
        \begin{block}{Agentic research is oxymoronic \arxiv{2608.31161}}
            \begin{itemize}
                \item Research, by definition, needs human creativity and
                    interpretation; an agent that writes the paper removes
                    exactly that.
                \item \keyterm{Deliteration}: a hollow literature, and
                    distrust of it.
                \item LLMs are for grunt work, which they ``usually get done
                    faster than I can do it myself.''
            \end{itemize}
        \end{block}
        \column{0.48\textwidth}
        \begin{block}{Where are the papers?}
            \begin{itemize}
                \item If autonomous agents could write good papers, they
                    could last year. They haven't.
                \item Not ``what if we prompted harder.'' If it doesn't
                    happen, it systematically won't.
                \item The real risk is to our people: they are so good at
                    grunt work that we stop doing the thinking.
            \end{itemize}
        \end{block}
    \end{columns}
    \vfill
    \begin{center}
        \textit{``If that's what most people think AI is, it's up to us to
        make a better narrative.''}
    \end{center}

\begin{frame}

    \framesubtitle{What the tool looks like when scientists design it}
    \vspace{-0.5em}
    \begin{columns}[T]
        \column{0.48\textwidth}
        \begin{block}{What the vendors are building}
            \begin{itemize}
                \item \texttt{@Claude} in Anthropic's own Slack; OpenClaw 2.0.
                \item An agent with the team's \keyterm{shared context} is a
                    multiplier, not one more participant.
                \item Fine, if your job is in Slack.
            \end{itemize}
        \end{block}
        \column{0.48\textwidth}
        \begin{exampleblock}{Where research actually happens}
            \begin{itemize}
                \item Alone at a desk. A whiteboard. Two laptops. Coffee.
                    A supervision. A meeting that is an event, not a channel.
                \item Make multiplayer mode \keyterm{ambient} at those
                    surfaces.
                \item Stage one drops the ``multi'': an AI you never touch
                    or look at.
            \end{itemize}
        \end{exampleblock}
    \end{columns}
    \vspace{0.5em}
    \begin{block}{Design rules from the room}
        There when I want it, never thrown at me. \quad
        Give me the numbers I need. \quad
        The device sits there, you behave as normal. \quad
        Nothing leaves the room.
    \end{block}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}
    \frametitle{The model was never the scarce thing}
    \framesubtitle{What the frontier vendors actually supply: a harness, and tokens priced below cost}
    \vspace{-0.5em}
    \small
    \begin{columns}[T]
        \column{0.48\textwidth}
        \begin{block}{Harness}
            The loop that reads files, runs code, checks results, asks
            again. Replaceable, and being replaced, by what a lab can build.
        \end{block}
        \begin{block}{Subsidised tokens}
            My own use ran at \$1,000 a day, about an ERC grant's worth.
            The reckoning comes when the price becomes honest.
        \end{block}
        \column{0.48\textwidth}
        \begin{exampleblock}{What we already run locally}
            Transcription. Retrieval. The LSST explanations. The exam. The
            arXiv digest. Editing a paper on quantum field theory: Gemma
            copes.
        \end{exampleblock}
        \begin{alertblock}{The threshold}
            When the whole chain, microphone to model to screen, is fast
            enough to join a conversation rather than be set running, the
            tool changes kind.
        \end{alertblock}
    \end{columns}
    \vfill
    \begin{center}
        Cambridge astronomy has built its own instruments for a century.
        If we don't build this one, we will be sold a puck.
    \end{center}
