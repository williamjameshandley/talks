\begin{frame}{An agent cannot do all the science in a paper}
    \framesubtitle{My rule of thumb: about 80\% of the work can be handed over}
    \begin{center}
        \begin{tikzpicture}
            \fill[C0!15] (0,0) rectangle (10.4,2);
            \fill[C1!25] (10.4,0) rectangle (13,2);
            \node at (5.2,1.35) {\Huge\textcolor{C0}{80\%}};
            \node[align=center] at (5.2,0.55) {Grunt work for agents};
            \node at (11.7,1.35) {\Huge\textcolor{C1}{20\%}};
            \node[align=center] at (11.7,0.5) {Human\\judgement};
        \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{center}
    \bigskip
    We decide what to calculate, and what to make of the answer: judgement and taste.

    \medskip
    Those choices were human in every example you've just seen.

    \bigskip
    A paper produced by agents from start to finish leaves that part undone.
    \par\bigskip
    \textcolor{C2}{\large In principle: $5\times$ faster, $5\times$ more fun.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusions}
    \framesubtitle{\tthref{handley-lab.co.uk}}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Agents do grunt work at scale.}\\
            In principle: $5\times$ more science and $5\times$ more fun.
        \item \textbf{The other 20\% is human judgement and taste.}\\
            Generating a paper does not do that work.
        \item \textbf{This work needs no frontier AI lab.}\\
            Made by scientists for scientists.
        \item \textbf{The agents should come to the room.}\\
            People work together at boards, in meetings and at conferences.
    \end{itemize}
    \medskip
    {\large\textbf{Ambient means AI that feels like electricity, not a digital scientist.}}
    \medskip
    \begin{center}
        \textit{Where in your working week would an agent listening to the room be useful?}
    \end{center}
